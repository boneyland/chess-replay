# Chess960: design

Approved roadmap item (docs/roadmap.md › Chess960), designed 2026-11-13. Chess960 is the only variant this player will ever support (decisions log, 2026-10-19).

## Why

The user wants three things with Chess960, all of them: replay their Lichess 960 games, set up and explore 960 positions, and play fresh 960 games against Stockfish or a friend. Today every one of these is refused: `ChessCore.readFen` rejects letter-file castling rights ("Chess960 positions aren't supported yet") and Import game rejects a PGN with `[Variant "Chess960"]`.

## Goals

1. **Read 960 positions and games.** A FEN with X-FEN or Shredder-FEN castling rights (`HAha`, `Kq` with the rook not on a/h, `BGbg`…) loads wherever a FEN does today (Import game's paste box, Edit position, Start from). A PGN with `[Variant "Chess960"]` (Lichess's export: `[FEN]` with `KQkq` meaning the outermost rooks, king off e1) imports and replays, with analysis, like a standard game. Rights in any order already read and still do.
2. **Start a fresh 960 game.** The Play vs human and Play vs computer dialogs' Start from gets a new choice, **Chess960**. Choosing it shows:
   - a **Random** checkbox, ticked by default;
   - a **position number** text field (0–959, Scharnagl numbering, as Lichess and Wikipedia use), greyed out (disabled) while Random is ticked. An out-of-range or non-number entry keeps Start disabled and says why.
   - Random picks uniformly from the 959 positions other than 518 (518 is the standard setup). Typing 518 by hand is allowed and gives the standard start.
   - Random is drawn when the game starts (on Start), not when the dialog opens.
3. **Explore a 960 position.** Menu › Explore gets **Chess960 position** with the same Random / number choice, opening a free board on it (both sides played by the viewer, Stockfish rating moves as on the free board today).
4. **Castling by the Chess960 rules** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess960#Castling_rules): after castling the king and rook stand where they would in standard chess (a-side: king c, rook d; h-side: king g, rook f). Legal only if: the right is held; every square between the king and its destination and between the rook and its destination is empty except for the king and that rook; the king is not in check, does not pass through and does not land on an attacked square. A castle may leave the king or the rook where it stands.
5. **Castling on the board, never by accident.** In a 960 position:
   - Two buttons near the board, **Castle a-side** and **Castle h-side**, each shown only while that castle is legal for the side to move and it is the viewer's move (never on Stockfish's turn). Wikipedia's rules recommend announcing a castle; the button is that announcement.
   - Dragging (or click-moving) the king to its castling square (c or g on its rank) while that castle is legal:
     - from two or more squares away, castles at once with no confirmation (the user's addition, 2026-11-13): a king can't move that far, so the drop can only mean castling, as in standard chess;
     - from one square away (a king step there could be meant), opens a small confirmation instead of moving: **Castle a-side/h-side** or **Move the king to c1** (only when that king move is also legal) and a way to cancel (the piece goes back). Choosing castle castles.
   - Dropping the king on its own castling rook castles at once, as on Lichess (the user's addition, 2026-11-13): a king can't otherwise move onto its own piece. This also covers a king already on its castling square (only the rook moves).
   - When a king move is the only legal reading of a drop, it moves as today.
   - Standard positions keep today's behaviour exactly (drag the king two squares to castle; no buttons, no confirmation).
6. **SAN and PGN.** Castles are written `O-O` (h-side) and `O-O-O` (a-side), as in standard chess and Lichess. Save PGN / My games / `PlayCore.pgn` for a 960 game write `[Variant "Chess960"]`, `[SetUp "1"]` and `[FEN]` with X-FEN rights (`KQkq` when the outermost rook is meant, the file letter only when an inner rook is). A position-number game also gets the number in Game info (*my call*: e.g. "Chess960 #612") and nowhere else.

## What "a 960 position" is

*My call*: no separate mode switch. A position is 960 when any castling right it holds (or, for a game, its start held) is not the standard king e1/e8 with rook a/h, or when the game came from a Chess960 PGN or the Chess960 start choice. Once a game or board is 960 it stays 960 for all its moves (the flag is kept on the game/board, stored in My games and free-board files), so the castle buttons and the engine's 960 mode don't flicker as rights are lost. **Position 518 plays as standard chess** (the user's decision, 2026-11-13): a Chess960 start that is the standard setup (typed 518, or a Lichess 960 game starting there) is not a 960 game: dragging the king two squares castles, no castle buttons or confirmation, Stockfish without `UCI_Chess960`, and the explorer, opening names and Ask Queen stay available. Only its saved PGN (`[Variant "Chess960"]`) and Game info ("Chess960 #518") say where it came from; so the game keeps a `chess960` number/label for those two, separate from the 960 flag that drives play.

## Edit position

*My call*: no 960 switch in the editor. The existing castling checkboxes (White O-O, O-O-O, Black O-O, O-O-O) become enabled whenever that side's king stands on its first rank with a rook of its colour on that side of it on the same rank; the right means the outermost such rook (X-FEN). The resulting position is 960 by the definition above if the king is off e or the rook off a/h. (Inner-rook rights can still arrive from a pasted Shredder or X-FEN.)

## Features in a 960 game

- **Stockfish** (lite, Full and asm): `setoption name UCI_Chess960 value true` before searching a 960 position (and false again for a standard one; the option is per worker, so it's set alongside Hash/Threads/MultiPV as they are). In 960 mode Stockfish writes castling as king-takes-rook (`e1h1`, `b1a1`); the page converts each way at the engine boundary. Best-move arrows for a castle point king → its destination square.
- **Lichess cloud eval**: used for 960 positions too, sent with `variant=chess960` and the X-FEN (checked 2026-11-13: the API documents both, and position 0's start answered at depth 42). Its castles stay king-takes-rook there (the page's 960 UCI), not converted to e1g1 as for standard positions.
- **Tablebase**: unchanged (tablebase positions never have castling rights).
- **Hidden in 960 games** (*my call*): the Lichess opening explorer panel and opening names (the explorer and `openings/` tables are standard chess only), and Ask Queen (its Space reads FENs with python-chess in standard mode and its model is trained on standard chess; checked 2026-11-13).
- **Dead positions**: chasolver gets the position with castling rights removed (they never decide a dead position); *verify* chasolver doesn't reject a 960 placement.
- **Repetition, fifty-move, results, clocks, takebacks, hints, critique, My games**: work as today; castling rights in a position's key are the rook files.
- **Video export, WebP, MP4, WebM**: the castle's rook and king both animate, from wherever they started.
- **Position from image**: unchanged (it never sets 960 rights).

## Settled by the builder check (*my calls*)

- Lichess game lists (`LichessCore.listGames`, Import game's Lichess list, `free-board.jsx` `can`) treat `Chess960` games as loadable; other variants stay refused.
- Edit position keeps an inner-rook right (from a pasted FEN) as it is while the viewer doesn't touch that checkbox.
- The castle buttons and confirmation also apply to the replay's own-move lines (`GameVideo` takes moves through `useBoardInput`) when the game is 960.
- Video exports never draw the castle buttons.
- Cache keys: X-FEN rights leave every standard position's key as it is today, so existing caches stay valid.

## Non-goals

- Any other variant (three-check, King of the Hill, crazyhouse, atomic, horde, antichess, racing kings).
- A 960 opening explorer or 960 opening names.
- Choosing different 960 positions for White and Black (Chess960 double / 921600).
- The Python renderer (`../chess-animator`) learning 960: `tests/check_browser_json.py` keeps checking standard games only.

## Anti-goals

- An accidental castle: a single drag or click must never castle in a 960 position without the confirmation or a button press.
- Any change to how standard games look or play.
- Mixing 960 and standard cached results: anything keyed by a FEN (engine caches, cloud and tablebase caches, dead-position cache) must not confuse a 960 position with a standard one that differs only in what its castling rights mean. Using rook-file rights in keys does this.

## Constraints

- Branch `claude/chess960`, small commits, PR against `main` (CLAUDE.md workflow); screenshots desktop 1440×900 and phone 390×844, Chromium and Firefox, on `pr-screenshots`.
- UI text follows the `ui-text` skill.
- Spec updates on completion: a new `docs/spec/chess960.md` (or sections in `free-board`, `import`, `engine`), remove the roadmap entry, a decisions-log line, contents line in `docs/spec.md`, part names in `docs/parts.md` for the castle buttons if they're a new part.

## Left to the builder

- The internal representation of castling rights (e.g. keep `castle` as `KQkq` with a per-position map of each right's rook square, or rook-file letters throughout), so long as every consumer (`CASTLE_HOME`/`canCastle` users in `edit-core`, `vision-core`, `dead.jsx`, `lichess-core`, `analysis-core`, `openings-core`, `game-video-core`'s `posKey`) keeps working.
- How the engine boundary converts castle UCI; where the castle buttons sit in each layout (desktop and phone) and the confirmation's look (the promotion picker is the obvious model).
- How the Start from dialog remembers the last Random/number choice (like the other Play choices).
- Test coverage: `chess-core` (FEN read/write both formats, castling legality incl. king/rook staying put, SAN both ways, the 960 numbering table: 518 = RNBQKBNR, 0 = BBQNNRKR), `import-core` (a Lichess 960 export fixture), `play-core` (PGN tags), `sf-engine` (UCI conversion), a live test that castles through the button and through the confirmation in Chromium and Firefox with real mouse input.
