# Share and export

[Spec](../spec.md) › what is built. The Share and export dialog, and the video and animated WebP exports it runs: how frames are recorded and drawn, and the formats.

## Share and export dialog

- **Share and export** (an icon beside Settings in the transport bar; in the phone's folded menu): a dialog in sections. **FEN**: the position on the board as the dialog opened, in a read-only field with a copy button. **PGN**: Download annotated (`ChessCore.annotatedPgn`: marks after the moves as they read (Qb7?!; other NAGs as $n), evals, clocks, the PGN's own comments and a notes file's beside them (the PGN's first, the same words once), Queen's answers, the PGN's variations as written, and with the analysis's evals (Game › Evaluations from) the engine's line on every move it didn't rate best (the Analysis panel's Best line after the move before; nothing after the last move) in place of the PGN's first variation there (a move never carries two better lines); on a move Go deeper searched, its deepest finished eval of the session in place of the analysis's, and its line on the next move in place of the analysis's line there, none where it starts with the move played, `withDeeper` and `ChessCore.withSearches`) and Download raw (`annotatedPgn(M, { raw: true })`: the tags and the moves alone), each with a copy button on its right; the free board has one Download (its moves, a game's clocks and result). **Notes** (only when the game has notes: a loaded notes file's, or the sample's own): Download notes and Copy notes, the game's notes as a `_notes.txt` notes file (`ChessCore.notesText`: [INTRO], [n] for the note after ply n, [CONCLUSION], each key on its text's line) that Import game reads back; an annotated PGN imported with its notes file exports the same again (`unsaid`: the notes the export wrote into the PGN's comments are taken back out). **Analysis**: Download analysis, for a browser analysis. **Export video**: the video/WebP export (the former Settings › Export tab); on the free board, Replay these moves as a game in its place. A copy button turns to a tick for 1.5 s; where the clipboard is refused the dialog closes and the note shows the text to select.

## Video and WebP export

How the video's page is sized while it plays and exports is in [Responsive layout](layout.md).

- **Exports record the page as it is, without the transport bar**: the screen-sized page with its margins as on screen (`stageMargins` in `app/engine.jsx`): as wide as the viewport (the stage's 16 px side padding, and one column's padding and scrollbar room) and the stage's padding above and below the page (12 px on a desktop; one column's 2 px above and 5 px below), all in the page's background, the page where it sits (nothing drawn past its own edge, as on screen); not the transport bar, its gap or the Offline tag. At `min(devicePixelRatio, 1920 / longer side)` (`LayoutCore.exportScale`), sides rounded down to even. A 1676 px window at 1× exports 1676 px wide. Settings' WebP widths are full (the default), the fixed widths below it, and half, and the exports use the text size chosen.
- **Export frames are drawn layer by layer** (`app/export-draw.js`): the stage splits into its layers (board, eval bar, player bars, each panel, the graph), each kept as a bitmap and redrawn only when its markup changes. The board, eval bar and graph (and any layer made only of boxes, images, single-line text and simple SVG) are drawn straight onto a canvas from the page's elements and computed styles; any other layer goes to html2canvas on its own. html2canvas costs ~65 ms a call even for one small element, ~0.3–0.5 s for the whole stage. Measured 2026-10-30 at 1440×900, five moves at 24 fps: 32–34 s before, 8–10 s after (Chromium and Firefox, MP4 and WebP). The board now keeps its drop shadow, which html2canvas left out. The live test `the export's own drawing matches html2canvas` checks those three layers are drawn natively and a whole frame is within 28 dB PSNR of html2canvas's. The camera push (when on) is in exports too: the page stays still while exporting and names the zoom on the box it would transform (`data-push`), and the compositor draws that box's layers zoomed, reusing their bitmaps; the same test checks a pushed frame against the still one zoomed.
- **Blurred box-shadows are worked out by the painter** (`blurredBox` in `app/export-draw.js`: a Gaussian-blurred rectangle, the product of two erf profiles), not by the canvas's `shadowBlur`: Firefox for Android draws that about three times too strong and cut off short (measured 2026-11-06 on Firefox Beta 160), which lit up the board and left bands in the panels' halos in a dark-theme export. Round corners of a few px are left square; a drop shadow on a picture (the pieces') still uses `shadowBlur`. A layer html2canvas draws (something in it the painter can't: Commentary's mask or a two-line comment, a cut-off name) gets its own box-shadow drawn under the capture by the compositor, as html2canvas draws only the box: at phone size those panels fell back on most frames and lost their halo.
- **MP4 frame rate**: constant by default (every frame `1/fps` long). Share and export › **Variable frame rate** (`mp4Vfr`, off; MP4 and WebM) skips frames whose markup didn't change (pauses, the title and end cards) and keeps the one before for their time (`videoEncoder` in `app/export.jsx`, the held picture copied to a second canvas); the frame rate is then the most a move's animation gets. The file is as long, with fewer, uneven samples (a 1440×900 title card to first move at 8 fps: 67 samples against 75, about the same size, as an encoder already spends almost nothing on a repeated frame). Measured 2026-10-30 on the first nine clips of the sample game at 8 fps (three runs each, after a warm-up export): the variable file is ~1.8% smaller and takes 0–10% less time (Chromium 5.4–5.7 s against 5.4–5.5 s, Firefox 6.3–6.8 s against 6.0–6.4 s), as the encoder skips the held frames; drawing them was already skipped in both. Live test `a variable frame rate MP4 holds unchanged frames` reads the `stts` box of both.
- **No MP4 or WebM without a video encoder**: both need WebCodecs' `VideoEncoder`, which Firefox for Android lacks (2026-11; Mozilla's bug 1746557). There Share and export offers only the animated WebP and says why (`VIDEO_EXPORT` in `app/export.jsx`), rather than failing once the export starts; the stored choice of MP4 or WebM stays for other browsers.
- **No WebP without WebAssembly SIMD**: the WebP's encoder (`webp_enc_simd`) needs it, as Stockfish's wasm does (`SFEngine.simdSupport`; Firefox on 32-bit Android lacks it), so there the WebP isn't offered and the dialog says why (`WEBP_EXPORT`); with no video encoder either, the Export video section says nothing can be exported here, in place of its settings.
- **WebM export** (Share and export › Export as › WebM): VP9 video and Opus sound in a WebM, from the same encoder path as the MP4 (`videoEncoder` in `app/export.jsx`, Mediabunny), with the MP4's quality setting (`mp4Quality`, labelled WebM quality there) and Variable frame rate. Offered only where `VideoEncoder.isConfigSupported` takes VP9 at 1080p (`WEBM_EXPORT`; not every browser's encoder does), and the frame's own size is checked again as the export starts. An MP4 export in a browser without an H.264 encoder still falls back to the same VP9 WebM. Measured 2026-11-06, Byrne–Fischer 10… Bg4 to 17… Be6!! (39 s) at 1440×900, 8 fps, High, after a warm-up export: Chromium 6.7 s (MP4 6.6 s), the WebM's video 2.56 MB against H.264's 1.77 MB; Firefox 28–29 s (MP4 8.6–8.8 s), 1.31 MB against 2.11 MB; the two files 50–53 dB PSNR apart. The dialog's note says Firefox takes about three times as long. Live test `a WebM export is VP9 with Opus sound` reads the file's EBML header and its two tracks.
