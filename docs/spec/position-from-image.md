# Position from image

[Spec](../spec.md) › what is built. Import game › Position from image, and the Play dialogs' Start from › From image.

## Position from image

Designed and built 2026-11-03 (plan: `docs/plans/2026-11-03-position-from-image.md`).

The viewer has a picture of a position: a Lichess or Chess.com screenshot, a diagram from a book or a web page, or a photo of a real board. They want it on the board without placing 20 pieces by hand. The app reads the picture on the viewer's device and opens the result in Edit position, with the picture beside the board for checking.

**Goals.** Any kind of chess picture in, a position in Edit position out, corrected there with the editor's tools. **Non-goals:** reading side to move, castling or en passant from the picture (no picture shows them); reading a game's moves; video or a live camera feed; dropping an image on the page (only the dialog takes images). **Anti-goals:** the picture going anywhere off the device; the model downloading before the viewer asks; Lite users and returning visitors paying for it (it never joins the service worker's per-deploy copy); a wrong read going straight to the free board unchecked.

**Model.** ChessQ Lite V4 (ViT-S, from [ChessQueries](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries), Joël Seytre, arXiv 2608.30762), the int8 ONNX build Lichess's website runs: `chessq-lite-v4-int8.onnx`, 41,370,398 bytes, SHA-256 `89d75e343c5d98f235855a67588efb469a4e7cff1e003ee6b6ee0de67732ae1f` (taken from `lichess-org/lila` `public/lifat/vision/`, a Git LFS file). Input `image`, float32 `[1, 3, 644, 644]` RGB, the whole crop squashed to 644×644 (no aspect kept, no padding), each channel `(v / 255 − mean) / std` with ImageNet's mean `[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]` and std `[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]`. Output `logits` `[1, 64, 13]`, squares a8…h8, a7…h7, …, a1…h1, classes `.PNBRQKpnbrqk`; argmax per square (first index wins a tie). It places pieces only and works out the board's orientation itself. Licence: PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 (the weights), which allows this non-commercial site with credit; the repo is then not all free software, as with the lisp sounds (README's Licence says so).

**What testing showed** (2026-11-03, native ONNX Runtime on this laptop's CPU, about 1.25 s per read; ~70 runs, positions read by eye for the photos):
- Lichess and Chess.com screenshots, three themes and flipped boards: 15 of 15 exact.
- Book and web diagrams: exact **once cropped to the squares**. The user's framed diagram (red frame, a–h / 1–8 labels, a move arrow) read as if seen from Black's side with stray pieces uncropped, and turned 180° with its frame kept; cropped to the squares, exact. An empty diagram with a label in every square came out with 13 invented pieces.
- Photos: best read whole or with a loose box (10 squares wrong across six near-start photos), worse cropped tight (21), worst with the box inside the board's edge (33) or the board warped to a square (30). Low, steep angles with pieces hiding each other fail whatever the crop. Sideways top-down boards read correctly.
- The model's own confidence flags most mistakes: squares under 80% top-class probability were the wrong ones in most failed reads.

**Hosting.** The model is committed to this repo in `vision/` with its `LICENSE` (PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0) and a `NOTICE` crediting ChessQueries and Lichess, pinned in LAYOUT.md like `vendor/`. Plain git, no LFS (41 MB is under GitHub's 100 MB file limit). Beside it is the runtime, onnxruntime-web 1.30.0's wasm build as Lichess uses it (`ort.wasm.min.mjs`, `ort-wasm-simd-threaded.mjs` and `.wasm`, 14.3 MB together, MIT, `ort.LICENSE`), vendored and pinned rather than loaded from a CDN (an `import()` can't carry an integrity hash, as with Mediabunny). `build.cjs` copies the four files (not the licences or `NOTICE`) to `vision/` next to the page, so they come from the site's own origin: no new host in the Content-Security-Policy, and PR previews and offline use work. They are not in `SW_LOCAL`. The Worker loads them from the cache `chess-vision-1` (filled only by the download below) as Blob URLs, typed as `VisionCore.FILES` says, so a page the service worker doesn't control (Shift+reload, service workers off) reads from the kept copy too, offline included (the Blob URLs are let go once the model is loaded, and a kept file of the wrong size drops the whole copy, the dialog saying so and offering the download again); `sw.js` also answers `vision/` requests from that cache or the network, and never keeps them itself, so the per-deploy `chess-replay-*` copy and its cleanup never touch them. Each first-time reader downloads 55.7 MB from Render, which counts against the site's bandwidth.

**Entry.** **Import game** › **Position from image** (Play/Explore › Explore until 2026-11-04); Cancel from picking or cropping goes back to Import game. From a game in progress Import game asks first (`guard`), as Edit position does. Hidden where `Vision.supported()` is false (no WebAssembly SIMD, `SFEngine.simdSupport()`, which the runtime needs, or no Workers, `OffscreenCanvas`, Cache Storage or `crypto.subtle`).

**The dialog** (`VisionDialog`, `app/vision.jsx`), step by step:
1. **First use: the download.** Before the files are kept the dialog says what the board reader is and that the picture isn't sent anywhere, and shows **Download board reader · 56 MB** (model plus runtime, 55,684,802 bytes, in decimal MB as Full Stockfish's 99 MB); nothing downloads before the click. The download is `KeptFiles` (`app/kept-files.js`), shared with Full Stockfish (`SFFull`, `app/sf-full.js`): progress by the megabyte with **Cancel the download**, given up after 30 s with nothing arriving, a storage-room check first, nothing kept unless every file arrives whole and matches its pinned SHA-256 (`VisionCore.FILES`), `navigator.storage.persist()`. Offline before the download, it says "The board reader downloads once; you're offline." and Download waits. A kept copy shows **Remove the board reader · 56 MB**, asking first, as Full Stockfish does.
2. **Picking the picture.** The dialog is 760 px wide with larger text than the other dialogs. Two tiles: a dashed **Drop an image or choose one** (Choose an image on a touch screen), which opens the file picker on a click, and **Paste image**. An image dragged over the tiles turns them into one dashed drop area ("Drop the image to read the board"), which stays while the file is held, still or moving, and goes once it leaves the tiles; dropped there, the first image is taken. While the dialog is open, files dropped anywhere else do nothing (the page's PGN overlay stays away). The crop step's picture takes a drop the same way, replacing the picture as a paste does. Choosing opens the file picker (`accept="image/*"`, which on a phone offers the camera too), and pasting an image (Ctrl+V / ⌘V, or the browser's Paste) works **only while this dialog is open** (pasted text is left alone). **Paste image** (beside Choose image, where the browser has `navigator.clipboard.read`) reads the clipboard on a click, so that pasting is plain to see: the first image there is taken as a paste would be; nothing but text there says "There’s no image on the clipboard."; a refused read (the browser's own prompt dismissed, or no permission) says "The browser didn’t let the page read the clipboard. Press Ctrl+V (⌘V on a Mac) here instead." The live test reads the clipboard in Chromium only: Firefox asks with its own Paste prompt, which Playwright can't press, so there the button is only checked for. A pasted or picked file that isn't a readable image says so ("That file isn't an image this browser can open.") and the dialog stays. The image's EXIF orientation is applied (`createImageBitmap(file, { imageOrientation: 'from-image' })`).
3. **The crop box.** The picture fills the dialog (drawn at its size on screen, never a full-resolution canvas) with a box over it, **starting on the board's squares** where a clean, upright 8×8 grid is found (computer-made and printed pictures; see [Board auto-crop](#board-auto-crop-and-orientation-from-labels)), **else around the whole picture**. The box has handles at its corners and edge midpoints (36 px to hit) and moves when dragged inside; outside it the picture is dimmed and inside it an 8×8 grid shows where the squares will be read. It's a plain rectangle, never a four-corner perspective warp (testing showed warping hurts photos). A line of help under it: "The box is on the board’s squares; drag its edges if it’s off." while the box is where a grid was found, else (none found, or the box pulled elsewhere) "A photo: read it as it is. A diagram or screenshot: pull the edges in to the squares." Pointer events, touch and mouse alike; from the keyboard the box is a Tab stop where Enter reads and Esc cancels. A picture pasted here replaces the one shown, the box found on it again. **Read board** and **Cancel**.
4. **Reading.** The box's part of the picture, scaled down first so its longest side is at most 2048 px (`VisionCore.cropRect`: a 48 MP phone photo is ~190 MB as RGBA, and the model sees only 644 px anyway), goes to a module Worker of the page's origin (`app/vision-worker.js`, through `Vision` in `app/vision.js`, one Worker per page, kept for later reads). The Worker squashes it to 644×644 the way Lichess's does (`OffscreenCanvas`, `imageSmoothingQuality: 'high'`; `ui/editor/src/editor.vision.worker.ts` in lichess-org/lila), normalises it and runs the model, with up to 4 threads where the page is cross-origin isolated, else one. "Reading…" shows meanwhile: on this laptop the first read (the Worker, the runtime and the model starting) takes about 4.5 s and later ones about 3 s; a phone is unmeasured. A failure (the runtime won't start, the Worker dies) says so in the dialog, the picture and box kept to try again. `VisionCore.read` turns the logits into a placement and a confidence per square.

**The result in Edit position.** Read board opens Edit position on the placement read, **White to move**, castling ticked wherever its king and rook stand on their home squares (the boxes' existing rule), no en passant, move 1. Everything Edit position does works as before (FEN field, tray, checks, Done disabled while a check line shows, Cancel going back to what showed before). It adds, only when opened from a picture:
- **The picture** (the cropped part, as a PNG object URL) stays on screen: in the two-column layout at the top of the Edit position panel, beside the board, in a box sized before the picture decodes (35% of the panel's height) that gives way when the panel's contents outgrow it (the unsure line, a check line, a FEN message), so Cancel and Done stay in the panel; in the one-column (phone) layout a **Hold to show picture** button lays the picture over the board while it is held (pointer down to up; Space held from the keyboard), so the board keeps its full size.
- **Turn board 180°**: turns the board as it stands, edits included (square *i* to 63 − *i*, `VisionCore.turn`), for a picture read from the wrong side; there is no separate copy of the original read. Unsure marks still showing turn with it.
- **Unsure squares**: each square whose top class is under 80% is outlined on the board, and the panel says "N squares to check (outlined)" ("1 square to check (outlined)" for one). A square's outline goes when that square changes, by the tray, a drag or a typed FEN; with none, the line doesn't show.
- **Back to crop** reopens the dialog at the crop box with the same picture and the box where it was, for another try (the crop decides the read: tight for diagrams, loose for photos). Read board then replaces the board and the marks with the new read, edits included, without asking; Cancel there returns to Edit position as it was. `GameApp` keeps the picture (an `ImageBitmap`) and the box in its editing state until the editor closes, and closes the bitmap and revokes the URL once nothing holds them.
- The picture and marks are not kept beyond that: Done, Cancel or a reload drops them; the stored board is the position, as from Edit position.

**From the Play dialogs**: Play vs human and Play vs Stockfish offer a third Start from choice, **From image** (where the board reader can run), whose button **Choose image…** opens this dialog while keeping the Play dialog's choices (time control, side), as Set up position… does. Its read opens the editor in that same mode: Done reads **Start game** and starts the game from the position, Cancel (in the editor, or in the dialog before any read) brings the Play dialog back as it was, and the picture, Back to crop and the marks work as above.

### Board auto-crop and orientation from labels

Designed and built 2026-11-03 (plan: `docs/plans/2026-11-03-board-auto-crop.md`). A diagram or screenshot reads exactly only once cropped to the squares, past any frame and coordinate labels, so the app finds the squares itself and opens the box on them, and uses the coordinate labels it crops away to make sure the read comes out the right way up.

**Goals.** For computer-made and printed pictures (screenshots of any site or app, web and book diagrams, scans), the crop box opens on the board's 64 squares, frame and labels outside it, adjustable with the handles as before. Where the picture has rank labels, the read's orientation follows them: a board shown from Black's side reads as such even when the model guessed otherwise. **Non-goals:** photos of real boards (they keep the box around the whole picture: testing found them read best whole or loosely boxed, worst cropped inside the board's edge); a check telling photos from other pictures (a photo that does show a clean, upright grid, such as a top-down board or a phone photo of a printed diagram, gets the box on its squares like any other: the user's call, as the poor photo results came from angled boards, which never pass the grid test); perspective or rotated boards; reading the labels as text for anything but orientation; side to move. **Anti-goals:** a wrong detection making things worse than today (anything short of a clean grid leaves the box around the whole picture); a message or button about either feature (the box simply opens in the right place, and the board is simply turned, without saying so: the user's call); a new download, host or file in the service worker's copy.

**Finding the squares** (`VisionGrid.findBoard`, pure, in its own unit `app/vision-grid.js`). When the dialog takes a picture (picked, or pasted, also at Back to crop), it draws a copy scaled so its longest side is at most 1024 px (never enlarged; *builder's call*, raise it if small boards in big scans need it) onto an `OffscreenCanvas` (`visionGrid` in `app/vision.jsx`) and passes its greyscale pixels to `findBoard({ g, w, h })`, which returns a box in fractions (`{ x0, y0, x1, y1 }`, as the crop box uses) or `null`, in about 40–60 ms on this laptop. Down each column and along each row it sums the rising and the falling steps between neighbouring pixels apart and takes their geometric mean: on a line between checkered squares both are large, at a frame's edge or the picture's border only one is. It looks for the 7 evenly spaced inner lines (spacing 8 px up, in quarter pixels, the best within a pixel of each, then a straight fit) over the whole picture, then each axis again over the band the other found, a square wider each side (a page's lines of text beside a small board otherwise pass for a lattice), the last pass strict: the weakest of the 7 peaks at least 0.35 × their median, that median at least 4 × the axis's median, the two spacings within 8%, the outer edges (a square beyond the first and last line) inside the picture with a pixel's slack. The greyscale copy that can't be made (a canvas that can't be read back) leaves the dialog as it was before this feature: the box around the whole picture and no orientation step. Last, the squares' brightness (the median of four patches in each square's corners, away from a centred piece) must alternate in a checkerboard: 56 of 64 squares, with at least 20 levels between light and dark. Anything else is `null` and the box opens around the whole picture as now, with no message. A board whose squares are all one colour (no checkerboard) is `null` too. The box opens on the outer grid lines, the squares' own edges, not the frame around them. Back to crop reopens at the viewer's last box, with the greyscale copy and the grid found kept from the first look (`GameApp` holds them with the picture), so nothing is found again.

**The help line** under the box: while the box is on the grid found, "The box is on the board's squares; drag its edges if it's off."; otherwise today's line ("A photo: read it as it is. A diagram or screenshot: pull the edges in to the squares.").

**Orientation from labels** (`VisionGrid.labelSide` and `VisionGrid.modelSide`, pure). At Read board, with the box as the grid, but only when the box is on checkered squares (`isGrid`: the same checkerboard test `findBoard` ends with), found or pulled in by hand. A box around a whole photo, or loosely round a photographed board, skips this step, so a photo's cells and a real board's printed border never turn a read:
1. **The labels.** Rank labels sit beside the board's left or right edge, just outside the box, or small inside the edge files' squares (Chess.com and Lichess's inside coordinates, near a corner of each square). The two ends of a rank-label column are "1" and "8"; an "8" carries clearly more ink than a "1" in any ordinary font, so comparing the ink of the glyph at the bottom end with the one at the top tells which side is at the bottom: White when "1" is at the bottom, Black when "8" is. Ink is a cell's pixels more than 60 levels from its median. Columns looked at: strips 0.05–0.7 of a square outside the box's left and right edges (pixel columns over 90% far from the strip's median, a frame, left out), each band of such columns at least 0.2 of a square wide on its own (labels printed in a wide frame, light on dark, as some sites' diagrams have them), and patches 0.3 of a square in each corner of the edge files' squares. Outside, each cell must hold one glyph: ink at most 0.45 of a square wide and 0.6 of a rank high, clear of the rank's top and bottom tenth, its middle within a quarter of the rank's, the 8 one above the other within 0.15 of a square (body text beside a board runs wide or fills the rank: in 200 made-up pictures of text 0.5 of a square from an unlabelled board, none answered). Inside, a blob of ink touching the patch's inner edges (a piece reaching into the corner) doesn't count. A label column has ink in all 8 cells and none over 3 × its median; where its "8" end carries at least 1.35 times the "1" end's ink (bold sans gives about 1.5), it answers `'w'` or `'b'`, the strongest column wins, and two columns that disagree give `null`. File labels aren't read.
2. **The model's side.** The model decides the orientation itself and gives no sign of it. `modelSide` compares which squares the picture shows occupied (`occupied`: a square whose middle half has a standard deviation over 14 levels; the middle only, so inside labels in the corners don't count) with the squares the read placed pieces on, taken as read and turned 180°. The way that wins must win clearly: at least 4 squares fewer wrong, and at most a third as many, else `null` (a placement nearly the same either way). Each misjudged square (an arrowhead, an invented piece) moves the difference by at most 2, so two can't flip the answer.
3. **Turning** (`shouldTurn`). When both answer and differ, the read is turned before Edit position opens: the placement by `VisionCore.turn` and the unsure squares by `VisionCore.turnSquares`, as Turn board 180° does. The picture beside the board is the crop as it was, not turned (as with Turn board 180°). Any `null` leaves the read as the model gave it. Nothing tells the viewer it happened.

The greyscale copy is kept with the picture in the dialog (and made again from the kept bitmap at Back to crop); nothing here touches the Worker or the model.
