# Responsive layout

[Spec](../spec.md) › what is built. How the page fits phones, tablets and desktops (`LayoutCore`).

## Responsive layout

Built 2026-10-26 (designed the same day), replacing the fixed video frames (1920×1080, compact, square, sideways and 840-wide portrait, scaled as a whole to the window) with a normal responsive page. Text keeps one comfortable size whatever the window, extra room goes to the board and the panels together, and nothing is cut off at any size (the anti-goal; a live test sweeps nine sizes for overflow).

- **Where it lives**: `LayoutCore` (`app/layout-core.js`, pure numbers in, boxes `{ x, y, w, h, scroll }` out) says where the board, eval bar, player bars and each panel go for the window's size, the view (`replay`, `free`, `game`, `edit`) and the text size. `Stage` (`app/engine.jsx`) measures the window, picks the column count and gives the views a frame `{ w, h, text, cols, video }` (`useFrame()`). Views place only what `LayoutCore` says; the board is a fixed 768 px picture scaled into its box (`BoardSpace`, `app/game-video-core.jsx`), so pieces, arrows and badges stay in board pixels through `view()`. The fixed `LAYOUTS`/`tallLayout`/`COMPACT`/`SQUARE`/`SHORT` frames are gone.
- **Two columns**: the board (with its player bars, and an eval bar in the replay and free board only) on the left, up to 900 px; on the right the game info, then Moves, Commentary under it, then Analysis. Moves wraps the moves in lines, as the Analysis panel's lines do (a light chip for White's move, a dark one for Black's) and is never taller than five lines (a third of the sidebar's room under the game info, at least its title and one line), scrolling by whole lines, its height rounded down to whole lines so none shows cut off; Commentary and Analysis share the rest. The eval graph (replay only) runs full width at the bottom (110 px in a viewport from 1181 px wide; 80 px in a narrower one) from 700 px high, and from 670 px wherever the board, held by the width, keeps its size with it there (as short as the room under the board, 80 px at least); otherwise it sits in the sidebar under Analysis (80 px) while the sidebar has the room, else it is hidden; under 670 px high it is always hidden. A square-ish two-column window (at least 0.77 times as tall as wide, as a 760 px wide window from about 640 px high) puts Analysis the full width under the board (at least 160·text px; the board gives way for it), and the eval graph at the bottom under it, full width, 80 px high (as in the sidebar), where Analysis keeps 240·text px over it (at 758 px wide, from about 836 px high), and the sidebar keeps the game info, Moves (40% of the room under the game info, still five lines at most) and Commentary. The sidebar takes at least 320·text px, and up to (230+150)·text+10 px while the board stays at 360 px or more, the width that once held Moves and Commentary side by side.
- **One column**: the board across the width, then the game info (the paused page only: a video has none), Moves (three lines at most), Analysis and Commentary at the bottom, at fixed heights (times the text size), each scrolling inside itself; the page scrolls and the transport bar stays pinned at the bottom of the screen. No eval graph (the eval bar stays, where the view has one). Edit position shows only its tray and panel, the tray under the board and the panel under that.
- **Which one**: `LayoutCore.columns()` picks one column when the window is tall for its width (height over 1.25 times the width, with hysteresis: to one column at 1.3, back to two under 1.2) or when the board beside the sidebar would be under 360 px (380 to come back): by the room left, not by device, so 1024×768 keeps the sidebar, 758×929 keeps it square-ish and 768×1024 stacks. A window too short for the one-column layout keeps two columns where a 200 px board still fits beside the sidebar (a phone held sideways, even at Larger text), and always for a video; a paused page in a narrow, short window (a phone with its keyboard up) takes one column.
- **Text size**: Settings › Appearance › **Text size** (Small, Default, Large, Larger: 90, 100, 115, 130 %; `TWEAK_DEFAULTS.textSize`, persisted). It sets the root size (`--text`, so the dock, Settings, menus and dialogs follow, all in `rem`) and the panels' text through `tx()`; widths that hold text (the sidebar minimum, the one-column switch, the bars) grow with it, so a larger size stacks sooner. The board's pieces don't scale. The browser's own zoom works too.
- **A video is screen-sized**: while playing or exporting, the page fits the window exactly (the one-column panels shrink under the board, to a 48 px floor, the board never under 200 px) and the title and end cards draw over it, so the size never changes mid-video. Panels follow the current part (Moves the current move), so a long comment can be out of frame at that moment.
- **Exports record the page as it is**: the screen-sized page at `min(devicePixelRatio, 1920 / longer side)` (`LayoutCore.exportScale`), sides rounded down to even. Settings' WebP widths are full (the default), the fixed widths below it, and half, and the exports use the text size chosen.
- **Export frames are drawn layer by layer** (`app/export-draw.js`): the stage splits into its layers (board, eval bar, player bars, each panel, the graph), each kept as a bitmap and redrawn only when its markup changes. The board, eval bar and graph (and any layer made only of boxes, images, single-line text and simple SVG) are drawn straight onto a canvas from the page's elements and computed styles; any other layer goes to html2canvas on its own. html2canvas costs ~65 ms a call even for one small element, ~0.3–0.5 s for the whole stage. Measured 2026-10-30 at 1440×900, five moves at 24 fps: 32–34 s before, 8–10 s after (Chromium and Firefox, MP4 and WebP). The board now keeps its drop shadow, which html2canvas left out. The live test `the export's own drawing matches html2canvas` checks those three layers are drawn natively and a whole frame is within 28 dB PSNR of html2canvas's. The camera push (when on) is in exports too: the page stays still while exporting and names the zoom on the box it would transform (`data-push`), and the compositor draws that box's layers zoomed, reusing their bitmaps; the same test checks a pushed frame against the still one zoomed.
- **Blurred box-shadows are worked out by the painter** (`blurredBox` in `app/export-draw.js`: a Gaussian-blurred rectangle, the product of two erf profiles), not by the canvas's `shadowBlur`: Firefox for Android draws that about three times too strong and cut off short (measured 2026-11-06 on Firefox Beta 160), which lit up the board and left bands in the panels' halos in a dark-theme export. Round corners of a few px are left square; a drop shadow on a picture (the pieces') still uses `shadowBlur`. A layer html2canvas draws (something in it the painter can't: Commentary's mask or a two-line comment, a cut-off name) gets its own box-shadow drawn under the capture by the compositor, as html2canvas draws only the box: at phone size those panels fell back on most frames and lost their halo.
- **MP4 frame rate**: constant by default (every frame `1/fps` long). Share and export › **Variable frame rate** (`mp4Vfr`, off; MP4 and WebM) skips frames whose markup didn't change (pauses, the title and end cards) and keeps the one before for their time (`videoEncoder` in `app/export.jsx`, the held picture copied to a second canvas); the frame rate is then the most a move's animation gets. The file is as long, with fewer, uneven samples (a 1440×900 title card to first move at 8 fps: 67 samples against 75, about the same size, as an encoder already spends almost nothing on a repeated frame). Measured 2026-10-30 on the first nine clips of the sample game at 8 fps (three runs each, after a warm-up export): the variable file is ~1.8% smaller and takes 0–10% less time (Chromium 5.4–5.7 s against 5.4–5.5 s, Firefox 6.3–6.8 s against 6.0–6.4 s), as the encoder skips the held frames; drawing them was already skipped in both. Live test `a variable frame rate MP4 holds unchanged frames` reads the `stts` box of both.
- **No MP4 or WebM without a video encoder**: both need WebCodecs' `VideoEncoder`, which Firefox for Android lacks (2026-11; Mozilla's bug 1746557). There Share and export offers only the animated WebP and says why (`VIDEO_EXPORT` in `app/export.jsx`), rather than failing once the export starts; the stored choice of MP4 or WebM stays for other browsers.
- **WebM export** (Share and export › Export as › WebM): VP9 video and Opus sound in a WebM, from the same encoder path as the MP4 (`videoEncoder` in `app/export.jsx`, Mediabunny), with the MP4's quality setting (`mp4Quality`, labelled WebM quality there) and Variable frame rate. Offered only where `VideoEncoder.isConfigSupported` takes VP9 at 1080p (`WEBM_EXPORT`; not every browser's encoder does), and the frame's own size is checked again as the export starts. An MP4 export in a browser without an H.264 encoder still falls back to the same VP9 WebM. Measured 2026-11-06, Byrne–Fischer 10… Bg4 to 17… Be6!! (39 s) at 1440×900, 8 fps, High, after a warm-up export: Chromium 6.7 s (MP4 6.6 s), the WebM's video 2.56 MB against H.264's 1.77 MB; Firefox 28–29 s (MP4 8.6–8.8 s), 1.31 MB against 2.11 MB; the two files 50–53 dB PSNR apart. The dialog's note says Firefox takes about three times as long. Live test `a WebM export is VP9 with Opus sound` reads the file's EBML header and its two tracks.
- **Video touches only while playing or exporting**: the title and end cards, the panels' fade-in, the blunder glow, the camera push and the commentary's typewriter. Stepping, clicking a move, exploring and analysing show a plain, still page, Commentary in whole (a long comment scrolls in its box). Pieces and arrows still slide to their squares, as normal app behaviour.
- **Every view**: the replay, the free board and explorer, Play vs human and Stockfish, and Edit position use the same layout. Overlays (dock, Settings, menus, dialogs) float over it as before, with their rules keyed on `.stage-one` in `app/shell.html`; `.stage-short` (a two-column window under 500 px tall) slims the transport bar.
- **Touch and drag**: the board's touch layer opts out of the page-wide drag-scroll (`data-no-drag-scroll`), so a mouse drag on the board moves a piece in the one-column page instead of scrolling it.
- **Tests**: `tests/layout-core.test.cjs` (boxes, the column rule, export scale) and the live layout sweep (`tests/live/site.test.cjs`: nine sizes from 320×568 to 2560×1080, both browsers, no text overflowing its box, the board's and panels' floors).
