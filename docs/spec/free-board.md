# Free board and games

[Spec](../spec.md) › what is built. The free board and what is played on it: the explorer, Edit position, Play vs human, Play vs computer, My games, and dead positions.

## Free board and explorer

A plain board with Stockfish, separate from the replay (`app/free-board.jsx`, state in `BoardCore`), reached from the dock's **Menu** (the Dock menu; on a phone the folded menu), in labelled groups: Explore (Starting position, Edit position), Play (Play vs human, Play vs computer, My games) and More (Keyboard shortcuts, Ask Queen). The button was Play/Explore until 2026-10-29.

- **Explore**: with the Analysis panel's Stockfish switch on (off for a new viewer since 2026-11-11), every move by either side gets Stockfish's verdict, as own moves do; the eval bar follows; Stockfish's eval and best line show before the first move too. Switched off, nothing is searched (Lichess's cloud evaluation, when on, still answers). Moves are numbered correctly from a FEN. Undo/redo with ← →.
- **Edit position** (below; it replaced Paste FEN on 2026-10-25).
- **Paste PGN** moved into [Import game](import.md#import-game) on 2026-11-04, which also takes a pasted FEN.
- **Copy FEN**: copies the six-field FEN of the position on the board, in the replay (a line being shown included) or on the free board; the half-move clock and move number count on from the start's. A note shows the FEN for 4 s; where the clipboard is refused it stays up, the FEN in a read-only field that selects it all with one click.
- **Opening explorer** in the Commentary box: the opening's name and the book moves that continue from the position (`openings-core.js` continuations), each clickable; a short note once out of book. No win rates or game counts (that is the [Lichess opening explorer](lichess.md#lichess-opening-explorer), built 2026-10-28, which takes this box's place when it's on). At 7 pieces or fewer, with the tablebase setting on, the endgame tablebase takes its place (see Endgame tablebase).
- **Replay these moves as a game** (Settings) opens the board's moves as a loaded game for replay, analysis and MP4, WebM or WebP export, with "Back to the free board" to return.
- **Back to the loaded game** (Settings › Game, shown on a free board) returns to the game left for it, paused at the move it was on: the loaded game's files are kept in the stored board as `lastGame` (survives a reload; one game only; loading another game, Back to sample game or Clear saved data drops it).
- The board (start FEN, moves, position in the line) survives a reload: `{ board: { fen, moves, upto, engine } }` in the stored files, `engine` the indexes of the moves played from Stockfish's reply chips (`byEngine`), so they are still labelled as its replies.
- A book or explorer move played by Enter or Space hands the focus to the next position's first row (`useRowFocus`), not the page.

## Edit position

The viewer sees a position or puzzle somewhere (a book diagram, a screen, someone's game) and wants it on the board, to analyse it or play on from it. Modelled on en-croissant's editor; the view is `EditBoard` (`app/edit-board.jsx`), its rules `EditCore` (`app/edit-core.js`), and `GameApp` swaps it in for the free board or the loaded game while its `editing` state is set (neither stays mounted, so no search, Stockfish reply or clock runs meanwhile).

- **Entry**: Menu › Explore › **Edit position**, under Starting position (the phone menu too). It opens on **the position on the board** (the replay's shown move, the free board's, or a game's; what Copy FEN gives), Empty clearing it; an empty board, White to move, only where there is none. From a game in progress it asks first (`guard`); the game is paused and kept in My games.
- **Layout**: the board where the free board draws it, centred where no eval bar stands beside it; the editor in the panels' room beside it in the two-column layout, with the piece tray in the panel. In the one-column layout the tray's two rows sit right under the board and the panel under them.
- **The panel**: a FEN field updated live, which sets up any well-formed FEN at once, legal or not (a `[FEN "…"]` tag too); only malformed text is refused, with a message under it, the board unchanged. **Start**, **Empty**, White/Black to move. Castling boxes (White and Black, O-O and O-O-O), each enabled only while its king and rook stand on their home squares; moving either off unticks it, and a pasted FEN's unsupported rights are dropped. **En passant** (None, or one of the squares the pieces allow: behind a pawn of the side not to move that could just have moved two, `EditCore.epSquares`; disabled where there is none), **Half-moves** (the half-move clock, 0 up) and **Move** (the move number, 1 up), typed fields that take a whole number up to 9999; other text is outlined in red with a line under it saying the old value stays (the FEN and Done keep it), and the setup's value shows again when the field is left. A double pawn push resets the clock, so choosing an en passant square sets the clock to 0, and a clock above 0 drops the square. The tray (White's six pieces, then Black's). A status line. **Cancel** and **Done**.
- **Editing**: drag a tray piece onto a square; or click/tap a tray piece to hold it (again, or Esc, lets it go) and tap squares to drop copies, a tap on the same piece taking it off. Drag a piece on the board to move it, or off the board to take it away. Squares through `view()`, so a flipped board works. An edit or a change of side to move drops the en passant square only where the pieces no longer allow it; the half-move clock and move number stay. All six FEN fields go on to the free board and a game started from the editor (Done, Start game, Position from image).
- **By keyboard**: the board is a Tab stop (`role="application"`). A tray piece picked with Enter or Space sends the focus to the board. There the arrows move a square outlined while it has the keyboard's focus (as the viewer sees the board, flipped too; it starts on e4); Enter or Space places the held piece (on the same piece takes it off), or with none held picks up the piece there and puts it down on the next square Enter is pressed on (Esc lets it go); Delete or Backspace takes the piece away; K Q R B N P place White pieces, k q r b n p Black ones. The keys are listed under the status line while the board has the keyboard's focus, and a screen reader hears the square and its piece.
- **Checks** (each a line in the status line, `EditCore.problems`): "White has no king." / "Black has 2 kings."; "The pawn on a8 can't stand on the first or last rank."; "Black is in check, but it's White's turn."; "White is in check from 3 pieces, which no move can give." / "… from two pieces in a line, …" (chessops' impossible-check rule, without its en passant cases). An empty board shows a hint instead ("Drag pieces from the tray, or tap one and then tap squares."). Done is disabled while any line shows; a setup with none is one `ChessCore.parseFen` accepts.
- **Done** opens the free board at the position (`commitBoard(BoardCore.fromFen(…))`). **Cancel** or Esc goes back to what showed before; a game left for the editor resumes, its clock running from then (the time spent editing isn't charged). A reload while editing drops the unfinished edit.
- Copy FEN copies the editor's FEN.
- **From the Play dialogs**: Play vs human and Play vs computer always offer Start from (This position where the board shows more than the start, Starting position, Set-up position, and From image where the board reader can run). With no position set up on the board, Set-up position turns Start game into **Set up position…**, which opens the editor keeping the dialog's choices (time control, side); there Done reads **Start game** and starts the game from the position, and Cancel brings the dialog back as it was.

## Play vs human

- Two players at one screen, from the starting position or a set-up position: the board's, or one set up in the editor from the dialog (Edit position). The board turns to the side to move after each move. No eval, arrows, verdicts, engine replies or book moves; Stockfish starts no search.
- Ends on checkmate, stalemate, insufficient material, a dead position behind locked pawns, resignation (asks first), a draw by agreement or a claimed draw, with the result in the Game box and Play again / Analyse there. As in FIDE's rules, a threefold repetition or fifty moves without a capture or pawn move let the side to move **Claim a draw** (the button replaces Offer a draw); a fivefold repetition or 75 moves end the game by themselves.
- **Stepping** (both kinds of game, in progress or finished): ← →, Home, End, the transport bar, the wheel and the Moves list show earlier moves (those before a game from a position too). While an earlier move shows, the board takes no move and a press on it goes back to the last one, as End does, with a note in the Game box saying so; clocks and the game go on by the last move, and Stockfish's reply (it plays on meanwhile) brings the board back to it.
- **Take back** is a button by Resign; no limit, nothing recorded. Time already spent stays used (a takeback never buys time); the move's increment comes off. Once a move has been played the clocks keep running, every move taken back too (`clock.begun`, kept over a reload and My games): before 2026-11-09 taking back every move stopped them as though the game hadn't started.
- **Time control** (optional, off by default): 5+3, 10+0, 15+10, 30+0, shown in the player bars; a flag fall loses unless the opponent has no mating material (draw). A reload charges the running clock for the time away (a flag can fall meanwhile); a game left (for another board or by closing the page) is kept in My games with the side to move's time so far used, and resumed from there with its clock running from when it opens (the time away isn't charged).
- Rules and clocks live in `PlayCore` (`app/play-core.js`); the game record is `board.game`, stored with the board.
- **Play from this position** (both Play dialogs): wherever the board shows a position (a loaded game at the move shown, an explored or own line in it, the free board, a finished or running game), Start from offers **This position**, first and chosen whenever it is more than the starting or set-up position. The game keeps the moves to it (those after the move shown are dropped) and plays on: `game.from` counts them. Take back never goes past them, the clocks start full there, Stockfish critiques only the player's moves after it, and repetitions count the earlier positions. The Moves list marks the first move of the game with a **GAME STARTS** pill; the PGN, My games ("from after 3… e6") and Play again keep the earlier moves.
- Leaving a game in progress asks first (and says the game is kept).

## My games

- Every game (`board.game.id`) is kept from its first move under `chess-video.games.v1` (`GamesCore`), the 50 newest, written on each move, takeback or result. A game with every move taken back stays while its clocks run (`PlayCore.begun`: a timed game that has begun); an untimed one leaves the list. Leaving a game with no moves on the board doesn't ask first. A stored list that can't be read is copied to `chess-video.games.v1.unreadable` (once, kept by Clear saved data) before the next write replaces it.
- The My games list resumes a game in progress or opens a finished one as a loaded game from `PlayCore.pgn` (replay, own moves, Analyse, Queen, Save PGN, video export). Each can be deleted. The crash screen's Clear saved data leaves the list alone.
- PGN headers: players, date, result, `[FEN]`/`[SetUp]` for custom starts, `[TimeControl]` and `%clk` when clocked.

## Play vs computer

- **Stockfish can't answer** (won't start, or stopped): the Game box says so with why, and **Try again** (beside Take back and Resign, "Try again, Take back or Resign") asks it again, so a player whose Stockfish dies before its first move, with nothing to take back, needn't resign (`useEngineOpponent`'s `retry`).
- The player picks White, Black or random; the board is drawn from the player's side and never turns. Stockfish plays at full strength (no strength setting: "prepare to get crushed") at the engine's `play` priority, after a short pause; its think time is capped so its clock doesn't flag.
- Same ends, Take back (back to the player's previous turn, Stockfish's reply included) and time controls as vs human, minus draw offers and claims: threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule end the game by themselves.
- **Hints**: on the player's turn the Game box has **Hint · 3 left**; a press shows Stockfish's best move as an arrow on the board and "Hint: Nf3 · 2 left" in the Game box, from the search Stockfish already runs on the player's turn (up to 5 s, "Stockfish is looking for a move…" until then). Three a game (`PlayCore.HINTS`), kept in the game record (`hints: [{ j, fen, best }]`) so a takeback or a reload never gives one back; back in the same position (after a takeback) its hint shows again for free. After the game the Commentary box says how many were used. Then "No hints left".
- Stockfish quietly rates the player's moves (`critique`: `{ j, cls, best, text }`); an inaccuracy, mistake or blunder is flagged in the Commentary box with a short line, and the better move shows only after the game (no evals, during it or after). A move is rated when the search before it and Stockfish's reply search both reach depth 15; short of that (a slow engine), only a blunder losing 300 cp or more (about a piece, or a mate) is flagged, once both reach depth 8 (`PlayCore.rateMove`).

## Dead positions with chasolver

Designed and built 2026-11-02 (plan: `docs/plans/2026-11-02-dead-positions.md`).

A position is dead when no legal sequence of moves mates either side (FIDE 5.2.2): the game ends there as a draw, at once. And a player whose flag falls loses only if the opponent could still mate by some legal sequence; otherwise it's a draw (6.9). Before chasolver, `PlayCore` proved only too little material (`canMate`) and pawns locked with kings and same-coloured bishops (`lockedDead`): a forced line into a dead position, or a lock with knights or rooks, played on, and the flag rule looked at material only. The endgame tablebase can't help: its "draw" means drawn with best play (KR v KR is one, yet either side can blunder into mate), and a TB win only proves a position isn't dead.

[chasolver](https://github.com/miguel-ambrona/chasolver) (Miguel Ambrona, MIT, a Rust crate, v3.0.0 of 2026-06-22; site https://chasolver.org) is the successor to his Chess Unwinnability Analyzer (CHA). Given a position and an intended winner, it finds a helpmate or proves none exists, and it has been run on every timeout of Lichess's standard rated games. It does the proving here.

**Measured** (2026-11-02): built for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` without changes (Rust 1.99.1) as a bare C-ABI wrapper crate. The page writes a FEN into a 256-byte buffer, and `solve(len, winner, fast)` returns unwinnable, winnable or undecided. It has no dependency besides the `chess` crate chasolver already uses, and is built with no imports, no wasm-bindgen, `lto`, `panic = "abort"` and `strip`. It is 1.05 MB (104 KB gzipped, mostly `chess`'s move tables) and instantiates in ~5 ms. In Node on the laptop it agreed with every classification in the crate's own test files, both sides of each position (137,900 checks, none wrong or undecided):

| Set | Full search: median / p99 / max | Fast check: max | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tests/lichess.txt`, 65,536 positions from Lichess games | 0.034 ms / 0.68 ms / 48 ms | 4 ms | 16 MB |
| `tests/positions.txt`, 3,414 hard community positions | 0.12 ms / 260 ms / 2.7 s | 2.6 s | 242 MB |

A search on every move of a real game is cheap, but rare constructed positions take seconds and grow the module's memory, which never shrinks. `is_unwinnable_fast` never called a winnable side unwinnable, but proved only 2% of the Lichess positions' sides, so it isn't used.

**Decided by the user:**

- **In a game** (Play vs human, Play vs computer): when chasolver proves that neither side can mate by any legal sequence, even with both cooperating, the game ends at once as a draw by dead position.
- **Flag falls follow FIDE 6.9:** the flag fall is a draw when chasolver proves the opponent can't mate, and a loss otherwise.
- **Loaded games and the free board point out dead positions too**, in all four of these ways (mockups 2026-11-02):
  1. **Analysis panel block**, under the engine lines like the Tablebase block: **Dead position · ½–½**, then "Neither side can mate by any series of legal moves (FIDE 5.2.2)".
  2. **Eval bar**: at even, its tag reading **DEAD** in place of the eval (as `TB 0.00` does). It takes precedence over a tablebase answer for the same position.
  3. **Moves list** (the replay and, since 2026-11-03, the free board): the first move after which the position is dead gets a small **DEAD** pill after its chip, except the last move when the list writes the result there (below).
  4. **One side only**: the same block says "**White can no longer win** · No series of legal moves lets White mate. Black still can." (or Black).
- **Stalemate comes first** (2026-11-03): a stalemate is also dead, but the game ends there by stalemate (FIDE 5.2.1) before the dead-position rule applies, as `PlayCore.outcome` already ordered it. A position without a legal move is never asked: on a stalemate the block reads **Stalemate · ½–½** ("Black has no legal move and is not in check (FIDE 5.2.1)") and the eval bar's tag **½–½**, never DEAD; a checkmate shows neither.
- **The fifty-move rule** (2026-11-03): on a loaded game (paused) and the free board outside a game, the Analysis panel shows a **Fifty-move rule** block (in the dead-position block's place and style; a dead position comes first) once the position's half-move clock, counted on from the start FEN's, reaches 100: "N moves without a capture or pawn move: the side to move can claim a draw (FIDE 9.3). The engine scores it a draw unless a capture or pawn move does better". At 150 it reads **75-move rule · ½–½**, drawn by itself (9.6.2). Nothing on a checkmate or stalemate (`PlayCore.fiftyRule`). Stockfish treats a count of 100 as a draw in its search, which is why a won-looking position read 0.00 with no word why.
- **The result after the last move** (2026-11-03), as other chess GUIs and PGN write it: the Moves list ends with the score and a short reason (`½–½ Stalemate`, `1–0 Checkmate`, `½–½ Dead position`, `0–1 White resigned`…), from `PlayCore.ending`. A game's own result; on the free board outside a game, the last position's own end only (checkmate, stalemate, insufficient material, a dead position); on a loaded game, the PGN's Result, with the final position's end as the reason where it gives that result, else the Termination tag's words (resignation, time, agreement, repetition), else who won (and a `*` game that ends in mate, stalemate or a dead position gets that). It shows while paused, and in the playing video (and its export) once the last move is played.

**Built with these calls (mine, unless the user decides otherwise):**

- **Never during the playing video or its export**, as the tablebase isn't: points 1, 2 and 4 show while the replay is paused and on the free board. The Moves pill is part of the paused page's list.
- **Not as hints in a game** (the anti-goal): during a game nothing shows until it ends. The game's end text says it: "Draw: dead position, neither side can mate", which replaces the locked-pawns wording. `resultText` and the PGN's `Termination` stay `normal`.
- **Undecided counts as not proven.** Nothing ends, shows or is marked on a guess. `canMate` and `lockedDead` stay as the synchronous first check (instant, and they cover the time before the module loads, or offline before it is kept). chasolver backs them up for what they can't prove.
- **Runs in a Worker** (`app/dead-worker.js`), so a slow position never freezes the board. Each question is a FEN plus the side; each answer is `unwinnable`, `winnable` or `undecided`. A page-side client (`app/dead.jsx` hook, with pure helpers in `app/dead-core.js`) caches the answers by position key (board, side to move, castling and en passant; the move counters don't matter). A search past 5 s ends with the Worker terminated and the answer `undecided`. After an answer that grew the module's memory past 64 MB, the Worker is restarted.
- **In a game**: after each move both sides of the new position are asked. `app/dead.jsx` installs the prover with `PlayCore.setProver`: a synchronous read of `DeadCore`'s cache (`true` unwinnable, `false` winnable, `null` not known), so `PlayCore` stays pure. `PlayCore.provenUnable(board, side)` is true when the prover says so of the current position or any earlier one in the game (a dead position stays dead after any legal move), and `outcome` makes a position where both sides are proven unable a draw (result ½–½, reason `dead`). The game isn't held up while the answer comes: when one lands after the move, `FreeBoard` calls `PlayCore.recheck(b, now)`, which ends the game by `outcome` on the current position, the side to move's clock charged to then and stopped; so a slow answer after more moves (the Stockfish reply, a quick premove) still ends it.
- **Flag**: `PlayCore.flag(b, now)` makes a flag fall a draw when the opponent has no mating material (`canMate`) or `provenUnable(b, opponent)`, else a loss; an answer not known yet counts as not proven. The answer is almost always in by then, since both sides are asked right after each move.
- **The replay**: when a game is loaded, its positions are asked in order in the Worker, both sides each, until the first one where both are `unwinnable`. That move gets the pill, and later positions are dead too, so they aren't asked. The position on the board while paused (including the viewer's own moves) is asked as it shows, for points 1, 2 and 4. On the free board outside a game, the position on the board is asked the same way.
- **Loading**: the module loads on a game's first move, a game's load in the replay or the free board's first position, never at page load. It's 104 KB gzipped (the Stockfish engine is 1.8 MB), so this is no heavier than the engine. `sw.js` keeps it like the rest of the page.
- **Build and pin**: the wrapper crate lives at `tools/chasolver-wasm/` (`Cargo.toml` pinning `chasolver = "=3.0.0"`, `Cargo.lock` committed, and the profile above). The built `vendor/chasolver.wasm` is committed and pinned by SHA-256 in LAYOUT.md with its build command and Rust version, and the site serves it next to the page (`VENDOR` in `build.cjs`). Render's build stays Rust-free; a test checks the committed file's hash against LAYOUT.md. The CSP needs nothing new (same origin); a Worker from the same origin falls under `worker-src`/`script-src 'self'`. The licence (MIT) goes in README with the vendored files.
- **chasolver's FEN**: `deadFen` (`app/dead.jsx`) keeps only the castling rights whose king and rook are still at home, since chasolver refuses a FEN whose rights don't fit the pieces (an edited start's might not). Its answers on a start position go through the same cache, so a game that starts dead ends as soon as the answer is in.
- **Caching**: the worker's undecided answers (gave up, ran past 5 s, a refused FEN) are cached for the visit, so a slow position isn't searched again; a worker that couldn't start or load its module isn't, so a later ask tries again; for 30 s after such a failure every ask answers undecided without starting a Worker (a page walking a loaded game's plies would otherwise start one per position, each failing, and Firefox reports each as a page error).
- **Tests**: Node unit tests load the real wasm and check a handful of positions from chasolver's test files (dead, one side unwinnable, winnable, a forced line into a dead position), `setProver` with `outcome`, `flag` and `recheck`, with and without the answer, `DeadCore`'s cache keys, and the first-dead-move scan. A live test covers a game ending dead in a position only chasolver proves (one picked from its test files: a lock with knights or rooks, or a capture forced into a dead position), the DEAD tag, block and pill on a loaded PGN, and a one-sided block on the free board.

Live test: `desktop: dead positions end a game, and show on a loaded game and the free board` (Chromium and Firefox), using `2b1k3/3N4/8/1p1p1p1p/1P1P1P1P/8/8/2B1K3 b - - 0 1`: after 1… Bxd7 the bishops are on both colours behind a full lock, which `canMate` and `lockedDead` don't prove.

**Open**: speed on a phone (**verify** on the Android phone; the Lichess set's p99 is under 1 ms on the laptop).
